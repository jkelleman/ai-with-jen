## Background

> Draft case study. Content and framing are still being refined.

A new AI data product needed a name and terminology system that could scale across surfaces, documentation, and onboarding. The team had many candidate names but no durable way to evaluate tradeoffs, and naming decisions were stalling downstream design and implementation.

## Data

1. 60 plus name candidates were in circulation across stakeholders.
2. 10 core naming constraints were identified and scored.
3. Research plan targeted 100 users across two personas.
4. Early signal from an MVP summit survey (N=24) shaped candidate refinement.

## Goal

Create a defensible naming decision and a reusable terminology process that improves clarity, reduces ambiguity, and can be reused for future launches.

## Role

Content Design Lead and Research Owner. I led naming framework design, study planning, and stakeholder alignment for the final recommendation.

## Action

1. Designed a constraint-based evaluation framework to compare name candidates consistently.
2. Built a research plan to validate candidates against user comprehension and confidence signals.
3. Synthesized survey and qualitative evidence into a decision-ready recommendation.
4. Aligned PM and design stakeholders on final naming direction and terminology rollout.

## Outcome

1. Broke naming deadlock and moved the team to a decision with clear evidence.
2. Reduced language ambiguity before launch by applying a shared evaluation model.
3. Established a repeatable naming method that can be reused across future product work.

## Learning

Naming decisions fail when treated as opinion debates. They succeed when constraints, user signal, and decision criteria are explicit and shared.

## Artifacts

1. Naming constraint matrix and candidate comparison model.
2. Terminology study brief and screener.
3. Recommendation summary used for cross-functional decision alignment.
