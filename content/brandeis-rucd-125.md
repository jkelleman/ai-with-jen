## Course Development Kit

The documents below contain the full course development kit — storyboarded as the actual Moodle experience students will navigate. Each week is broken into 6 sequential pages with student-facing text, video scripts, activities, and completion triggers.

### Reading Order

| # | Document | What it covers |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | Course Overview | Course at a glance, architecture, learner journey, weekly model |
| 2 | Learning Architecture | CLOs, objective-to-activity map, 8-week table, assessment |
| 3 | Week 1 Storyboard | Full storyboard (6 pages, 4 video scripts) |
| 4 | Week 2 Storyboard | Full storyboard (6 pages, 4 video scripts) |

## Week Storyboards

<div class="brandeis-accordion">

<button class="brandeis-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
  <span class="toggle-icon">+</span> Week 1: Foundations — What AI Actually Is
</button>
<div class="brandeis-accordion-content" hidden>

**Learning Objectives:** Distinguish between AI's public narrative and its technical reality; identify the assumptions designers carry about AI and evaluate them against evidence.

**Core Readings:** Shneiderman *Human-Centered AI* Ch 1–3; Mitchell *Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans* Ch 1–2, 8

**6-Page Structure:**

1. **Welcome to Week 1** — Intro video (5–15 min). Sets the frame: this course treats AI as design material, not magic.
2. **Reading: Shneiderman** — *Human-Centered AI* Ch 1–3. Reading guide video. Focus: the HCAI framework as an alternative to the autonomy-vs-control binary.
3. **Reading: Mitchell** — *AI Guide* Ch 1–2, 8. Reading guide video. Focus: what AI actually does vs. what people think it does.
4. **Industry Spotlight** — Curated articles: NNG AI-Powered UX, MIT Sloan on AI strategy, a16z/Sequoia on AI markets. Memes included.
5. **Exercise: AI Assumption Audit** — Write 3 things you believed about AI before the readings, check each against the evidence, and identify one assumption that changed. Post to forum.
6. **Wrap-Up** — Key takeaways video (5–10 min). Trust rating baseline (1–5 scale) — revisited in Week 8 capstone.

</div>

<button class="brandeis-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
  <span class="toggle-icon">+</span> Week 2: Trust, Mental Models, and Cognitive Load
</button>
<div class="brandeis-accordion-content" hidden>

**Learning Objectives:** Apply the 18 HAX Guidelines to evaluate a real AI product; explain how mental models and cognitive load shape whether users trust AI.

**Core Readings:** Amershi et al. HAX Guidelines (CHI 2019); Norman *The Design of Everyday Things* Ch 1–2

**6-Page Structure:**

1. **Welcome to Week 2** — Intro video. Transition from "what AI is" to "how users experience AI."
2. **Reading: HAX Guidelines** — Full 18-guideline framework. Reading guide video. Focus: which guidelines are easy to meet and which reveal hard trade-offs.
3. **Reading: Norman** — *DOET* Ch 1–2. Reading guide video. Focus: mental models, affordances, and how AI disrupts them.
4. **Industry Spotlight** — Case studies: NNG AI Mental Models, NNG AI Trust, NNG Chatbot Personality. Supplementary: Kahneman *Thinking, Fast and Slow*.
5. **Exercise: HAX Guideline Mapping** — Choose an AI product, evaluate it against all 18 HAX Guidelines, identify the 3 biggest gaps, and propose design changes. Post to forum.
6. **Wrap-Up** — Key takeaways video. Preview of Week 3.

</div>

<button class="brandeis-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
  <span class="toggle-icon">+</span> Week 3: GenAI as Design Material
</button>
<div class="brandeis-accordion-content" hidden>

**Learning Objectives:** Distinguish between cyborg and centaur models of human-AI collaboration; design for both paradigms and articulate the trade-offs.

**Core Readings:** Mollick *Co-Intelligence* Ch 2–3, 6; NNG AI and UX series

**6-Page Structure:**

1. **Welcome to Week 3** — Intro video. Frame: GenAI changes the design material. The medium is no longer static.
2. **Reading: Mollick** — *Co-Intelligence* Ch 2–3, 6. Reading guide video. Focus: cyborg (tight integration) vs. centaur (division of labor) models.
3. **Reading: NNG AI/UX Series** — 5 NNG articles on AI interaction patterns. Reading guide video. Focus: when GenAI adds value vs. when it creates noise.
4. **Industry Spotlight** — Case studies on prompt design, AI writing assistants, and Copilot-style interfaces. Supplementary: Khosla, Maeda.
5. **Exercise: Cyborg vs. Centaur Design Challenge** — Choose a design task. Design it twice: once as a cyborg workflow (human+AI interleaved) and once as a centaur workflow (AI does X, human does Y). Compare trade-offs. Post to forum.
6. **Wrap-Up** — Key takeaways video. Preview of Week 4.

</div>

<button class="brandeis-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
  <span class="toggle-icon">+</span> Week 4: AI-Augmented Research
</button>
<div class="brandeis-accordion-content" hidden>

**Learning Objectives:** Apply AI responsibly as a research partner across one phase of the design research process; evaluate the trade-offs of AI-augmented research methods.

**Graded Deliverable:** AI Research Partner Brief (Applied Exercise 1 of 3, worth 40% total)

**Core Readings:** Lew & Schumacher *AI for UX*; Portigal *Interviewing Users* Ch 1, 3, 5

**6-Page Structure:**

1. **Welcome to Week 4** — Transition from design skills to research practice. Where can AI genuinely help in design research, and where does it introduce risk?
2. **Reading: Lew & Schumacher** — AI's role in user research workflows. Focus: which research phases benefit from AI and which require human judgment.
3. **Reading: Portigal** — Foundations of qualitative research. Frame as the human baseline that AI can augment but not replace.
4. **Industry Spotlight** — NNG AI-Moderated Interviews, AI Survey Writing, Synthetic Users, Don't Outsource Analysis.
5. **Graded: AI Research Partner Brief** — Design a research plan using AI as a partner for ONE phase. Must include: research question, method, AI's specific role, guardrails, and what stays human.
6. **Wrap-Up** — Key takeaways. Mid-course check-in survey embedded.

</div>

<button class="brandeis-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
  <span class="toggle-icon">+</span> Week 5: Ethics, Bias, and Responsible AI
</button>
<div class="brandeis-accordion-content" hidden>

**Learning Objectives:** Apply Crawford's supply chain framework to audit an AI system for bias; classify AI systems using the EU AI Act risk framework.

**Graded Deliverable:** Bias Audit (Applied Exercise 2 of 3)

**Core Readings:** Crawford *Atlas of AI* Ch 1–3, 6; Costanza-Chock *Design Justice* Ch 1–2

**6-Page Structure:**

1. **Welcome to Week 5** — The hardest week. Bias in AI is not a technical bug to fix — it's a systemic issue baked into data, design, and deployment.
2. **Reading: Crawford** — Supply chain framework for understanding AI. Where does the data come from? Who labeled it? Whose labor is hidden?
3. **Reading: Costanza-Chock** — Centering marginalized communities in design. How "designing for everyone" often means "designing for the default."
4. **Industry Spotlight** — PAIR Explorables, Stanford HAI, MS RAI Standard v2.1, EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF. Supplementary: Broussard *More Than a Glitch*.
5. **Graded: Bias Audit** — Choose an AI system. Map its supply chain (Crawford), classify it (EU AI Act risk tiers), identify 3 bias risks, propose design interventions.
6. **Wrap-Up** — Key takeaways. Preview of Week 6.

</div>

<button class="brandeis-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
  <span class="toggle-icon">+</span> Week 6: Transparency, Explainability, and Trust at Scale
</button>
<div class="brandeis-accordion-content" hidden>

**Learning Objectives:** Design tiered transparency for different user expertise levels; apply Shneiderman's governance frameworks to a real AI system.

**Graded Deliverable:** Three Levels of Disclosure (Applied Exercise 3 of 3)

**Core Readings:** Shneiderman *HCAI* Ch 7–9; Ammanath *Trustworthy AI* Ch 1, 3–4, 8

**6-Page Structure:**

1. **Welcome to Week 6** — Transparency is not a checkbox. Showing users a 500-word explanation of how the model works is not transparency.
2. **Reading: Shneiderman** — Governance, oversight, and institutional structures for trustworthy AI. Returns to HCAI with deeper focus on systemic trust.
3. **Reading: Ammanath** — Industry perspective on building trust at scale. Practical frameworks for organizations implementing AI.
4. **Industry Spotlight** — PAIR Explorables, NNG AI Chatbot Guidelines, Sensible AI (CHI 2025), NNG GenAI for Complex Questions.
5. **Graded: Three Levels of Disclosure** — Choose one AI feature. Design transparency for 3 audiences: novice, power user, expert/auditor. What's shown, what's hidden, why.
6. **Wrap-Up** — Key takeaways. Group office hours available.

</div>

<button class="brandeis-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
  <span class="toggle-icon">+</span> Week 7: Accessibility, Inclusion, and AI Agents
</button>
<div class="brandeis-accordion-content" hidden>

**Learning Objectives:** Identify AI-specific accessibility risks that traditional WCAG guidelines don't cover; evaluate how AI agents change the user-system relationship.

**Core Readings:** Treviranus selected writings; W3C AI Accessibility; NNG AI Agents as Users

**6-Page Structure:**

1. **Welcome to Week 7** — AI creates new categories of accessibility challenges that traditional web accessibility guidelines were not designed to address.
2. **Reading: Treviranus** — The leading voice on inclusive design and AI. Her framework for "designing at the margins" — start at the edges, not the average.
3. **Reading: W3C + NNG** — AI-specific accessibility working documents and what happens when AI agents are the ones using interfaces.
4. **Industry Spotlight** — MS Inclusive Design Toolkit, Stanford HAI mental health AI study, NNG AI Literacy. Supplementary: Hendren *What Can a Body Do*.
5. **Exercise: Accessibility Risk Audit** — Choose an AI feature. Identify 3 AI-specific accessibility risks not covered by WCAG. Describe the risk, who it affects, and a design intervention.
6. **Wrap-Up** — Key takeaways. Preview of Week 8 capstone.

</div>

<button class="brandeis-toggle" aria-expanded="false">
  <span class="toggle-icon">+</span> Week 8: The Designer's Manifesto (Capstone)
</button>
<div class="brandeis-accordion-content" hidden>

**Learning Objectives:** Synthesize course frameworks into a personal design philosophy for AI; articulate and defend design principles grounded in evidence.

**Graded Deliverable:** Designer's Manifesto (Capstone, 25%)

**Core Readings:** Agrawal *Power and Prediction* Ch 1–6; NNG AI Agent Definition

**6-Page Structure:**

1. **Welcome to Week 8** — The capstone week. Seven weeks of vocabulary, evaluation, design, auditing, and transparency — now synthesize into something you carry forward.
2. **Reading: Agrawal** — The economics of AI. How prediction machines change decision-making. The copilot-to-agent shift.
3. **Reading: NNG Agents** — What agents are, how they differ from copilots, and the design implications. Supplementary: Monteiro *Ruined by Design*.
4. **Industry Spotlight** — Current state of AI agents in production. Real-world deployment successes and failures.
5. **Capstone: Designer's Manifesto** — Write 5–7 design principles for human-centered AI. Each must: state the principle, cite a course reading, give a concrete example, acknowledge a tension. Callback: revisit Week 1 trust rating.
6. **Course Wrap-Up** — Final reflection video. Trust rating comparison (Week 1 vs. Week 8). End-of-course survey. Resources for continued learning.

**Capstone Format:** 1,500–2,000 words. Rubric criteria: principle clarity, evidence grounding, practical specificity, tension acknowledgment, trust reflection.
